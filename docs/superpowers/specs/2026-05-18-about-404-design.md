## Why this exists

`web/src/components/BroadsheetNav.tsx:17` already declares an `/about`
link target. Today, following that link lands on a router 404 with no
treatment — the page just disappears off the broadsheet shell. Two
gaps need closing before the Atlas can ship a deployed beta to
pokers (per the cluster plan, this is the parallel frontend win for
the launch-readiness cluster):

1. **`/about` page.** Mission, methodology, criteria, acknowledgments.
   Single-column `.page` treatment matching the existing inner-page
   vocabulary (Browse, Metro, Results all use `.page`).
2. **Newspaper-style 404.** A typed, on-brand fallback when the
   router can't match a URL. Today a missing route just renders the
   masthead and an empty `<main>`.

The contract is purely a UI add — no openapi, no wire types, no API
calls. The work is two new route components, a new `errorElement` on
the root route, and one nav-link verification.

## Strategic goal

Close the visible UX gap in the broadsheet shell so a deployed beta
feels finished, not in-progress. Stay strictly within the established
design vocabulary — no new dependencies, no new CSS unless the
broadsheet language genuinely lacks the affordance.

## Design

### 1. Routes & pages

#### `/about` — `web/src/routes/About.tsx`

Single-column `.page` layout (already defined in `global.css:447`).
The component is a pure-content function — no `useQuery`, no router
hooks, no state. Just the broadsheet-shaped article.

Structure:

```tsx
<div className="page">
  <header className="page-header">
    <h1>About the Urbanist Atlas</h1>
    <p>{lede}</p>
  </header>
  <section>
    <h2>Mission</h2>
    <p>...</p>
  </section>
  <section>
    <h2>Methodology</h2>
    <p>...</p>
  </section>
  <section>
    <h2>Criteria for inclusion</h2>
    <p>...</p>
  </section>
  <section>
    <h2>Acknowledgments</h2>
    <p>...</p>
  </section>
</div>
```

The four `<h2>` tags pick up the existing `.page h2` styling
(`global.css:489-498`): small-caps, amber tagline color, letter-spaced.
The `<section>` rule (`global.css:484-487`) adds a top border between
sections automatically. **No new CSS required.**

Copy target: ~450 words total. The maintainer will iterate copy after
merge; the goal here is a substantive starter, not a final draft.

**Lede paragraph** (after `<h1>`, inside `.page-header`):

> A directory of transit and safe-streets advocacy organizations,
> searchable by US ZIP or Canadian postal code. A companion volume to
> [*Urbanist Lexicon*](https://mjrossi.com).

**Mission section:**

> The Urbanist Atlas exists for a narrow reason: when someone moves
> to a new city — or wakes up one morning angry about a dangerous
> intersection three blocks from home — they should be able to find
> the people already organizing for better streets and better transit
> nearby, in under a minute.
>
> National advocacy outfits do plenty of good work, but they are easy
> to find on their own. The harder search is for the neighbourhood
> committee, the metro-area rider alliance, the county-level Vision
> Zero coalition. The Atlas indexes those.

**Methodology section:**

> Entries are curated by hand. Each organization in the Atlas was
> reviewed by an editor against the criteria below before being added.
> There is no algorithmic ingestion and no automated scraping; the
> directory grows one editorial decision at a time.
>
> The underlying dataset is licensed under the
> [Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/)
> and will be available for download once the API exits its initial
> dogfooding phase. Public submissions are coming with the API-key
> program; until then, suggestions are welcome by email.

**Criteria for inclusion section:**

> The Atlas indexes organizations whose primary work is **transit
> advocacy** (riders' alliances, bus/rail coalitions, transit
> committees) or **safe streets advocacy** (Vision Zero groups,
> neighbourhood traffic-calming coalitions, pedestrian and cycling
> alliances).
>
> Out of scope, deliberately: housing and YIMBY organizations, even
> when their priorities overlap. The directory's job is to be useful,
> not exhaustive, and a tighter scope makes the local-search
> experience cleaner. Consultancies, think tanks, and academic centres
> are also out of scope unless they double as a membership advocacy
> organization.

**Acknowledgments section:**

> Postal-code geography in the United States comes from the
> [U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov)'s ZIP Code Tabulation
> Areas (ZCTAs); Canadian postal-code geography comes from
> [Statistics Canada](https://www.statcan.gc.ca)'s Postal Code
> Conversion File. Both are public-domain or open-licensed.
>
> Above all, this directory is built on the work of the organizations
> it indexes — the volunteers, organizers, and staff who show up to
> their city's transportation meetings, week after week, and patiently
> argue for better.

#### `/*` (errorElement) — `web/src/routes/NotFound.tsx`

Newspaper-style 404, wired as `errorElement` on the root route. Reuses
the `.page` layout so the masthead/nav/footer chrome stays present.

Structure:

```tsx
<div className="page">
  <header className="page-header">
    <h1>Page not in this edition.</h1>
    <p>
      <em>The story you were looking for could not be found in our
      directory.</em>
    </p>
  </header>
  <section>
    <p>
      We retract the link, with apologies. The page you tried to reach
      either never went to press or has since been pulled from the
      directory.
    </p>
    <p>
      <Link to="/" className="not-found-return">Return to the front page</Link>
    </p>
  </section>
</div>
```

**Why one new CSS class.** The "RETURN TO HOMEPAGE" small-caps link
is a treatment the broadsheet vocabulary doesn't already cover —
`.section-label` is small-caps but is for headings, not interactive
text; `entry-name a` is the underline-on-hover article-title style,
also wrong. The new `.not-found-return` class lives in
`global.css` (one rule, ~10 lines): small caps, amber tagline color,
letter-spaced, underline. Named with a `not-found-` prefix so it's
obvious where it belongs and easy to delete if the 404 page is
later restyled. The single-purpose class scoped to the slice avoids
muddying the more general `.section-label` token.

**Why `errorElement`, not a `*` route.** `react-router` v7's
`errorElement` on the root route catches both 404s (no route matched)
**and** thrown errors from any descendant route's loader/component.
That's the same coverage a `*` catch-all gives for 404s, but it also
provides a graceful fallback for unexpected errors elsewhere in the
app — so it's a strict superset. The router comment at line 14
already anticipates "a shared not-found / error page" landing here.

### 2. Router wiring

`web/src/router.tsx` gains one new field (`errorElement`) on the root
route and one new child route (`/about`):

```ts
export const router = createBrowserRouter([
  {
    path: '/',
    Component: App,
    errorElement: <NotFoundWithLayout />,  // NEW
    children: [
      { index: true, Component: Home },
      { path: 'r/:postalCode', Component: Results },
      { path: 'browse', Component: Browse },
      { path: 'm/:metroSlug', Component: Metro },
      { path: 'about', Component: About },  // NEW
    ],
  },
]);
```

**Subtlety:** `errorElement` on the root route renders **instead of**
the root route's component (i.e., `App`), so the masthead/nav/footer
chrome from `App` is NOT included by default. To keep the chrome
visible on 404 — which is what the newspaper-style treatment needs —
the error element must wrap `<NotFound />` in the same layout that
`App` provides. `NotFoundWithLayout` is a tiny composition:

```tsx
function NotFoundWithLayout() {
  return (
    <>
      <Masthead />
      <BroadsheetNav />
      <main>
        <NotFound />
      </main>
      <Footer />
    </>
  );
}
```

This lives in `router.tsx` itself (or alongside `NotFound`) — pulling
it into its own file would be premature given it's three lines.
Defined locally in `router.tsx` next to the route table is fine.

The comment at `router.tsx:13-14` is updated to reflect that
`errorElement` is now wired.

## Non-goals (explicit)

- **No new dependencies.** Per CLAUDE.md, no react-helmet, no
  remark, no MDX. Inline JSX copy is fine.
- **No CSS module migration, no new layout classes beyond
  `.not-found-return`.** The single utility class is the only addition.
- **No backend changes.** Slice is frontend-only.
- **No roadmap status update.** Per slice instructions, the roadmap
  `Status` block stays untouched in this PR.
- **No new component extraction.** `NotFoundWithLayout` stays inside
  `router.tsx`; About has no internal sub-components. The components
  are simple enough that breaking them up would be premature.
- **No internationalization scaffold.** Hard-coded English copy.
- **No SEO/meta tags.** `<title>` and `<meta>` per-route work is a
  separate concern (no slice for it on the roadmap yet).
- **No 404-page analytics or referrer logging.** Out of scope.
- **No copy decisions reviewed with the maintainer.** The brief said
  "write a substantive starter; the maintainer will edit/iterate
  after merge." Copy lands as-is and is iterable in follow-up PRs.

## Risks & mitigations

- **`errorElement` swallowing real exceptions silently.** If a
  descendant component throws a real bug, the user gets a 404 page,
  not a stack. Mitigation: this is the same trade-off every SPA
  makes; React's dev-mode error overlay still surfaces the underlying
  exception during development. Production-only error telemetry is
  out of scope for v1.
- **Copy aging poorly.** The maintainer wrote the brief as a starter
  intended to be edited after merge — explicitly accepted.
- **`/about` link target staying broken if router edits are missed.**
  Mitigated by the `About.test.tsx` test that clicks the nav link and
  asserts the page rendered. (Sub-test, not the primary coverage.)
- **`.not-found-return` styling drift if reused outside the 404.**
  Mitigated by the class name's specificity (`not-found-` prefix);
  if a similar treatment ends up wanted elsewhere, the right move is
  to lift it into a generic `.smallcap-link` token at that point, not
  to retroactively rename.
